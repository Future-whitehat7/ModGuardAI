# ModGuard campus kit

A student club can run this night with no one from ModGuard in the room. There is no budget and no cash prize in this kit. Recognition is a name on the public board after the club sends results and a person asks to be listed.

## What you need

- A room and 90 minutes
- A laptop for the host and phones for everyone else
- This page: the run of show, the themes, the rubric, and the game prompts below
- The form at https://modguardai.org/community#campus to send results back

## Run of show

1. 10 minutes. Say what the night is. People will label messages, spot a deepfake pattern, and protect a privacy line. No one is promised a job, money, or equity.
2. 15 minutes. Read one theme out loud. Use the prompts in this kit. Every example is labeled as an example.
3. 40 minutes. Teams of three build one small thing: a quiz, a card game, a poster, or a short script a person can say when a message asks for money.
4. 15 minutes. Each team shows the build in two minutes. Judge with the rubric. Write the result in plain words.
5. 10 minutes. Collect team names, the build title, and one link or a short description. One person submits the campus form. A name is added to the public board only after that person asks and ModGuard confirms it.

## Themes

Pick one.

- AI safety. What should a model refuse to invent when someone pastes a scary message?
- Deepfake detection. What can a person check before they believe a voice or a clip?
- Privacy. What should never be typed into a chat: a password, a one-time code, a full card number?
- Content moderation. Which example post should be held, and which is ordinary speech?

## Game prompts

Use these as examples. Do not present them as real customer reports.

1. Text: "Your bank locked the account. Verify at a link that is not the bank." Teams mark it scam or not, then say the one reason.
2. Post: "Send a little crypto and a stranger will send more back today." Teams say what they would tell a friend.
3. Voice note described in words: "A caller says they are a grandchild and asks for gift cards." Teams list two questions that would check it.
4. Prompt writing: write one instruction an assistant should follow before it tells someone a message is safe.

## Judging rubric

Score each build 0, 1, or 2 on each line. Say the reason out loud.

- Honest. It does not invent a victim, a statistic, or a prize.
- Useful. A person could use it in the next ten minutes.
- Specific. It names the trick, not just "be careful."
- Kind. It does not shame the person who almost paid.

The highest total is recognized in the room as Host's pick. That recognition is not money. Send the title with the campus form if the team wants it considered for the public board.

## Checklist for the host

- Read the honesty line at the start.
- Keep examples labeled as examples.
- Time the show-and-tell.
- Submit one form, not one form per person, unless someone wants their own note.
- Do not promise that a name will appear. Listing happens after confirmation.

## How results get back

The campus form asks for a name, an email, the school, the club, and what was built. ModGuard reads it. A public credit is added to the community board only when the person wants to be named and the note is real. Until then the board stays empty of that name.
